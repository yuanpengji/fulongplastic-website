## PC组培瓶灭菌后瓶口变形问题，如何通过现场排查找到真正原因？

## 引言

在植物组织培养生产过程中，高压灭菌是培养容器使用前非常重要的一个环节。

无论是PC组培瓶、PP培养容器，还是其他培养体系，都需要经历高温、高压环境，以满足后续无菌培养要求。

但在实际生产过程中，偶尔会遇到一些现场问题。

例如：

- 部分培养瓶经过高压灭菌后出现瓶口轻微变形。

当类似问题发生时，很多人的第一反应通常是：

- 是不是培养容器质量问题？
- 是不是材料耐温性能不足？
- 是不是灭菌参数设置异常？

这些判断都很正常。

但对于商业化生产来说，真正的问题原因往往需要结合现场情况，通过系统排查才能确定。

近期，我们就遇到了一次PC组培瓶高压灭菌后瓶口变形的问题。

通过多次现场测试和排除分析，最终找到了真正影响变形的因素。

## 客户反馈：部分PC组培瓶灭菌后出现瓶口变形

客户在生产过程中发现：

部分PC组培瓶经过高压灭菌后，瓶口位置出现轻微变形。

客户将异常样品寄回后，我们第一时间进行了检查。

从变形状态来看：

瓶口受到外部压力作用导致变形的可能性较高。

因为PC材料本身具有较好的耐温性能，在正常高压灭菌条件下，如果产品状态正常，一般不会出现明显变形。

为了进一步确认原因，我们没有仅通过样品进行判断，而是决定直接前往客户生产现场进行排查。

## 第一次排查：检查组培筐与培养瓶摆放方式

到达客户现场后，我们首先检查了整个灭菌流程。

客户使用的高压灭菌设备：

- 共7层结构；
- 每层放置培养容器；
- 总共放置28摞培养容器。

现场观察发现：

组培筐与PC组培瓶之间存在一定间隙，并不是完全贴合。

因此我们首先考虑：

是否由于组培筐匹配度不足，在堆叠过程中导致部分瓶口受到集中压力？

如果摆放过程中受力不均，上层重量可能会传递到部分瓶口位置，从而造成局部变形。

于是我们进行了第一次调整：

- 重新整理组培筐摆放方式；
- 尽量保证培养瓶排列整齐；
- 降低局部受力情况。

但重新测试后：

瓶口变形问题依然存在。

这说明：

单纯调整组培筐摆放方式，并不能解决问题。

## 第二次排查：调整灭菌结束后的排气时间

排除了摆放因素后，我们继续分析其他可能原因。

高压灭菌结束后的排气过程，也是塑料培养容器容易受到影响的环节。

如果排气速度过快，灭菌设备内部压力快速变化，可能会对部分培养容器产生影响。

因此我们进行了第二次测试。

将原来的排气时间：

由6分钟调整到12分钟。

通过延长排气时间，降低压力变化速度。

但测试结果显示：

底部部分培养瓶仍然出现瓶口变形。

通过这一步，可以基本判断：

排气速度并不是主要影响因素。

## 第三次排查：排除透气系统影响

为了进一步排除其他因素，我们又针对透气系统进行了检查。

考虑到透气膜可能影响瓶内外气体交换，因此我们对透气状态进行了调整。

测试结果显示：

即使改变透气条件后，底部区域的部分PC组培瓶仍然出现变形。

至此：

透气膜因素也基本可以排除。

## 最终发现：灭菌设备底部支撑结构存在问题

经过多次测试后，我们重新回到最初的判断：

瓶口变形仍然更符合外部受压导致的情况。

于是我们进一步检查灭菌设备内部结构。

最终发现：

高压灭菌锅底部支撑板存在异常。

由于设备刚开始放置少量重量时，底板变化并不明显。

但当整个灭菌过程装载较多培养容器后：

- 底部受力增加；
- 支撑板出现轻微下沉；
- 导致部分区域承受额外压力。

最终造成底部位置的PC组培瓶瓶口受到挤压，从而出现变形。

更换新的底部支撑板后，再次进行测试：

瓶口变形问题消失。

## 为什么生产问题需要系统排查？

在实际生产过程中，人们往往会优先关注最容易看到的问题。

例如：

- 瓶子变形 -> 怀疑瓶子质量；
- 盖子异常 -> 怀疑盖子设计；
- 培养污染 -> 怀疑培养容器。

但实际上，生产过程中的问题往往涉及多个环节。

可能来自：

- 产品本身；
- 灭菌设备；
- 操作方式；
- 摆放结构；
- 配套工具。

因此，解决问题不能只根据表面现象判断，而需要通过排除法逐步定位真正原因。

## How to Identify the Real Cause of Deformation in PC Tissue Culture Bottles After Autoclaving: A Customer Site Investigation Case

## Introduction

In commercial plant tissue culture production, autoclaving is an essential step for preparing culture vessels before inoculation and cultivation.

PC tissue culture bottles, PP culture containers, and other tissue culture vessels are commonly required to withstand high-temperature and high-pressure sterilization conditions before entering the production process.

However, during actual production, some practical issues may occasionally occur.

For example:

- Some culture bottles may show slight deformation around the bottle mouth after autoclaving.

When this type of problem appears, the first questions are usually:

- Is there a problem with the bottle quality?
- Is the material performance insufficient?
- Are the autoclaving parameters incorrect?

These are reasonable considerations.

However, in commercial production environments, the real cause of a problem often cannot be determined only by looking at the final result. A systematic investigation of the entire process is usually required.

Recently, we encountered a case where a customer found deformation on the bottle mouth of PC tissue culture bottles after autoclaving.

Through multiple on-site inspections and elimination tests, we were able to identify the actual factor affecting the deformation.

## Customer Feedback: Bottle Mouth Deformation After Autoclaving

During routine production, the customer noticed that some PC tissue culture bottles developed slight deformation around the bottle mouth after autoclaving.

After receiving the samples from the customer, we immediately inspected the affected bottles.

Based on the deformation characteristics, our initial judgment was:

The bottle mouth deformation was more likely caused by external pressure rather than a material issue.

PC material itself has good temperature resistance and is widely used in reusable tissue culture applications. Under normal autoclaving conditions, properly manufactured PC tissue culture bottles should maintain stable performance.

However, instead of making a conclusion only from the samples, we decided to visit the customer’s production site and investigate the complete operating process.

## First Investigation: Checking Culture Basket Arrangement

After arriving at the customer’s facility, we first reviewed the autoclaving process.

The customer’s autoclave system included:

- 7 loading layers;
- 4 stacks arranged per layer;
- 28 stacks of culture containers in total.

During the inspection, we noticed that there was some space between the culture baskets and the PC tissue culture bottles.

Therefore, our first assumption was:

Could uneven support during loading cause concentrated pressure on certain bottle mouths?

If the culture baskets and bottles are not positioned properly, the weight from upper layers may transfer unevenly and create pressure points on individual bottles.

Based on this possibility, we adjusted the loading method:

- Reorganized the culture basket arrangement;
- Improved the alignment of the bottles;
- Tried to make the pressure distribution more uniform.

However, after another test cycle:

The bottle deformation still appeared.

This indicated that the arrangement method alone was not the main cause.

## Second Investigation: Adjusting the Exhaust Time After Autoclaving

After excluding the loading arrangement factor, we continued analyzing other possible causes.

Another possibility was the pressure release process after autoclaving.

If the pressure changes too quickly after sterilization, plastic containers may experience stress caused by rapid pressure differences.

Therefore, we performed a second test.

The exhaust time was extended:

From 6 minutes to 12 minutes.

By slowing down the pressure release process, we aimed to reduce possible stress on the containers.

However, the test result showed:

Some bottles at the bottom area still developed deformation.

Based on this result, we could basically exclude exhaust speed as the main factor.

## Third Investigation: Eliminating the Influence of the Breathable Membrane

To further investigate the issue, we continued checking the ventilation system.

Since the breathable membrane affects gas exchange between the inside and outside of the container, we considered whether the ventilation condition could influence the deformation.

Therefore, we adjusted the breathable membrane condition and performed another test.

The result showed:

Even after changing the ventilation condition, deformation still appeared on some bottles located at the bottom area.

At this stage:

The breathable membrane factor could also be excluded.

## Final Finding: The Problem Was Related to the Autoclave Support Structure

After several rounds of testing, we returned to the original observation:

The deformation pattern was still consistent with external pressure.

Therefore, we carefully inspected the internal structure of the autoclave.

Finally, we found that:

The bottom support plate inside the autoclave was not completely stable and had an uneven condition.

When only a small amount of weight was placed inside the autoclave, the issue was difficult to notice.

However, when the autoclave was fully loaded:

- The total weight increased;
- The bottom support area received higher pressure;
- The support plate slightly moved downward in certain areas.

As a result, some bottles located at the bottom experienced additional pressure, causing deformation around the bottle mouth.

After replacing the bottom support plate and repeating the test:

The deformation issue was no longer observed.

## Why Is Systematic Investigation Important in Production?

In actual manufacturing environments, people often focus first on the most visible problem.

For example:

- Bottle deformation -> suspect the bottle quality;
- Cap issue -> suspect the cap design;
- Contamination -> suspect the culture vessel.

However, production problems often involve multiple connected factors.

Possible causes may include:

- The product itself;
- Sterilization equipment;
- Operating procedures;
- Loading methods;
- Supporting equipment.

Therefore, solving a production issue requires more than identifying the visible symptom.

A step-by-step elimination process is often necessary to locate the real cause.
