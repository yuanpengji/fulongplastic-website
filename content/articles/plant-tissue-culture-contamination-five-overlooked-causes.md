## 为什么植物组织培养频频出现污染？5个容易忽略的关键问题

植物组织培养污染是影响实验室研究、商业化微繁殖及种苗生产稳定性的常见问题之一。

在实际培养过程中，有时接种操作看起来一切正常，但经过数天培养后，培养基开始浑浊，瓶内出现菌丝，甚至同一批次的培养材料陆续受到污染。

污染不仅会造成培养材料损失，还可能增加人工、培养基、容器周转及生产管理成本。

然而，植物组培污染通常并非单一因素造成。外植体处理、无菌操作、培养容器、培养基灭菌及培养室管理，都可能影响污染发生的风险。

本文将从五个常见环节出发，介绍植物组织培养污染的潜在来源，以及实验室可以采用的初步排查和预防方法。

## 一、外植体处理不到位

外植体是植物组织培养的重要污染来源之一，尤其是在初代培养阶段。

植物材料的表面可能附着细菌、真菌等微生物，部分植物组织内部还可能存在内生微生物。

即使经过常规表面消毒，也不一定能够完全排除污染风险。

此外，不同植物来源、取材部位、生长阶段及采集季节，都可能影响外植体的污染情况。

**建议：**

- 加强母本植株的日常管理。
- 根据植物种类优化取材部位和预处理流程。
- 记录不同来源材料的污染情况。
- 对反复污染的材料，进一步排查潜伏性内生微生物。

## 二、无菌操作存在细节风险

即使实验室配备了超净工作台和灭菌设备，不规范的操作仍然可能引入污染。

常见问题包括：

- 接种工具灭菌不充分。
- 操作动作不规范，阻挡或扰乱洁净气流。
- 手套、工作台面或工具受到污染。
- 培养容器开启时间过长。
- 污染材料未及时隔离，造成交叉污染。

对于商业化组培实验室而言，人员和批次数量较多，更需要统一操作规范。

**建议：** 建立并执行标准操作流程（SOP），规范工具处理、工作台清洁、接种操作和污染材料管理，减少人为操作带来的风险。

## 三、培养容器与瓶盖配套不当

培养容器不仅用于盛装培养基和植物材料，还需要在培养期间维持适宜的内部环境，并提供相应的物理屏障。

培养瓶、培养盒、组培袋及其配套的瓶盖或封口结构，都是培养体系的重要组成部分。

### 1. 瓶盖匹配度与密封可靠性

不同培养容器的瓶口尺寸、螺纹及配套盖型可能不同。

如果瓶盖与容器匹配不当，可能出现盖合不到位、松动或密封不可靠等情况。

因此，采购和使用培养容器时，应确认瓶身与瓶盖的适配性。

### 2. 透气膜的完整性

对于带透气膜的培养容器，膜片需要在提供适当气体交换的同时，保持相应的微生物阻隔功能。

如果透气膜出现破损、脱落或固定不牢，可能影响其屏障完整性。

日常使用中，应检查透气膜是否完整、固定是否可靠。

### 3. 广口与窄口培养容器的区别

广口培养容器通常具有更大的操作开口，便于放入或取出植物材料。

但在开盖接种过程中，更大的开口可能增加培养基和植物材料暴露于操作环境的面积。

因此，在其他条件相近时，广口容器需要更加注意开盖时间及无菌操作。

这并不意味着广口容器的污染率一定高于窄口容器，实际结果仍然取决于操作流程、封口状态和培养条件。

### 4. 额外封口措施

对于部分螺纹盖培养容器，实验室可能会在瓶盖与瓶身的连接处增加一层适用的封口膜，以加强封口管理。

但额外封口是否适用，需要考虑容器结构、膜材、灭菌条件及气体交换要求。

封口膜不能替代合适的瓶盖，也不能保证完全避免污染。

**建议：** 优先选择瓶身与盖子匹配可靠、透气膜完整的培养容器，并根据具体培养体系评估是否需要额外封口。

## 四、培养基灭菌不充分

培养基通常含有糖类、无机盐及其他营养成分，在支持植物组织生长的同时，也可能为微生物提供适宜的繁殖条件。

因此，培养基灭菌是污染防控的重要环节。

即使灭菌设备设置为121℃，也不代表所有培养基都已经获得充分灭菌。

常见问题包括：

- 装载过满，影响蒸汽流通。
- 容器排列过密，导致受热不均。
- 灭菌设备排气或运行异常。
- 不同培养基体积采用相同灭菌时间。
- 热敏性成分过滤除菌后添加时，发生二次污染。

**建议：** 根据培养基体积、容器类型及设备条件验证灭菌流程，并设置未接种的空白培养基对照，帮助排查培养基制备、灭菌和后续处理过程中的问题。

## 五、培养室管理容易被忽视

污染防控不仅发生在接种阶段，也需要贯穿后续培养过程。

培养室管理中值得关注的问题包括：

- 培养架及环境清洁不到位。
- 已污染的培养材料未及时隔离。
- 培养容器或封口状态未定期检查。
- 批次记录不完整，难以追溯污染来源。

需要注意，培养室环境存在微生物，并不意味着微生物一定能够进入完整、屏障有效的培养容器。

但如果容器存在破损、封口异常或后续开盖操作，环境管理就可能成为需要重点排查的因素。

**建议：** 保持培养室清洁，定期检查培养容器状态，及时处理污染材料，并建立完整的批次管理和追溯记录。

## 六、出现污染后，如何初步排查？

不同的污染表现，可以为排查提供线索。

| 污染表现 | 建议优先检查方向 |
| --- | --- |
| 接种后短时间内集中污染 | 外植体处理、接种操作、培养基灭菌 |
| 同一来源材料反复污染 | 母本管理、取材部位、潜伏性内生微生物 |
| 不同材料在同一批次污染 | 操作人员、工具、工作台及共用物料 |
| 未接种的空白培养基出现污染 | 培养基制备、灭菌、封装及后续处理 |
| 瓶口或盖子附近出现异常 | 容器配套、封口状态及接种时的操作 |
| 培养较长时间后才出现污染 | 潜伏性污染、早期低水平污染及后续操作 |

以上仅为初步排查方向，不能单独根据污染时间或位置确定污染来源。

必要时，应结合培养记录、对照试验及适当的微生物检测进一步分析。

## 七、如何降低植物组织培养污染风险？

植物组织培养污染往往是多个环节共同作用的结果。

对于实验室及商业化组培基地，建议重点建立以下管理机制：

1. 规范母本管理与外植体处理。
2. 建立标准化无菌接种操作流程。
3. 检查培养容器、瓶盖及透气膜的完整性。
4. 验证培养基灭菌流程并设置适当对照。
5. 加强培养室管理与批次追溯。

污染防控的核心，并不是依赖某一种设备或耗材，而是通过系统化管理降低各环节的风险。

## 结语

植物组织培养是一项对操作环境、材料处理和生产管理要求较高的技术。

无论是科研实验室还是商业化微繁殖企业，稳定的培养结果都离不开规范的无菌操作、可靠的培养容器及持续的过程管理。

当污染反复发生时，通过记录、对照和逐步排查寻找原因，通常比单纯调整某一个环节更加有效。

**组培生产的稳定性，来自整个培养流程的规范管理。**

## Why Does Contamination Occur in Plant Tissue Culture? 5 Commonly Overlooked Causes

Contamination is one of the recurring challenges in plant tissue culture laboratories, commercial micropropagation facilities, and plant propagation operations.

In some cases, cultures appear normal immediately after inoculation, but the medium becomes cloudy or fungal growth develops inside the container several days later.

Contamination can result in the loss of plant material and additional costs associated with labor, culture media, container handling, and production management.

However, contamination rarely has only one possible source. Explant preparation, aseptic technique, culture containers, medium sterilization, and culture room management can all influence contamination risk.

This article examines five commonly overlooked areas and outlines practical approaches to contamination prevention and initial troubleshooting.

## 1. Inadequate Explant Preparation

Explants are an important potential source of contamination, particularly during culture initiation.

Plant material may carry bacteria, fungi, and other microorganisms on its surface. Some microorganisms may also occur internally as endophytes.

Routine surface disinfection cannot necessarily eliminate microorganisms located within plant tissues.

Contamination risk may also vary depending on the source of the plant material, sampling location, growth stage, and collection season.

**Recommended practices:**

- Maintain healthy stock plants and appropriate stock plant management procedures.
- Optimize sampling locations and pretreatment methods for the plant species.
- Record contamination patterns associated with different explant sources.
- Investigate possible latent endophytic contamination when repeated problems occur.

## 2. Hidden Risks in Aseptic Operations

Even laboratories equipped with laminar flow workstations and sterilization equipment can experience contamination when aseptic procedures are not followed consistently.

Common issues include:

- Inadequate sterilization of inoculation tools.
- Improper movements that obstruct or disturb clean airflow.
- Contaminated gloves, work surfaces, or tools.
- Leaving culture containers open longer than necessary.
- Delayed isolation of contaminated cultures, increasing cross-contamination risks.

For commercial micropropagation facilities, consistent procedures become especially important as the number of operators and production batches increases.

**Recommendation:** Establish and follow standardized operating procedures (SOPs) covering tool preparation, workstation cleaning, inoculation practices, and contaminated material handling.

## 3. Poor Compatibility Between Culture Containers and Lids

Culture containers do more than hold culture media and plant material. They also help maintain a suitable internal environment and provide a physical barrier during cultivation.

Culture bottles, boxes, bags, and their corresponding lids or sealing systems are important parts of the overall culture setup.

### 3.1 Lid Fit and Sealing Reliability

Different culture containers may have different neck dimensions, thread designs, and compatible lid specifications.

An unsuitable lid may result in incomplete closure, looseness, or unreliable sealing.

Laboratories should therefore confirm compatibility between the container and its lid before use.

### 3.2 Vent Membrane Integrity

For vented culture containers, the membrane should support appropriate gas exchange while providing the required microbial barrier.

Damage, detachment, or insecure attachment may compromise membrane integrity.

Membrane condition and attachment should be checked as part of routine container inspection.

### 3.3 Wide-Mouth vs. Narrow-Mouth Containers

Wide-mouth containers generally provide a larger opening, making it easier to insert or remove plant material.

However, during open-container inoculation, a larger opening may expose a greater area of the culture medium and plant material to the working environment.

Therefore, wide-mouth containers may require additional attention to opening time and aseptic handling.

This does not mean that wide-mouth containers always have higher contamination rates. Actual outcomes depend on operating procedures, sealing conditions, and the culture system.

### 3.4 Additional Sealing Measures

For some screw-cap culture containers, laboratories may apply an appropriate sealing film around the lid-to-container junction as an additional sealing measure.

However, its suitability depends on container design, film material, sterilization conditions, and gas exchange requirements.

Additional sealing film cannot compensate for an incompatible lid or guarantee contamination-free cultures.

**Recommendation:** Choose containers with reliable lid compatibility and intact vent membranes, and evaluate additional sealing measures according to the requirements of the culture system.

## 4. Insufficient Sterilization of Culture Media

Culture media typically contain sugars, mineral nutrients, and other components that support plant tissue growth but may also support microbial growth.

Effective medium sterilization is therefore essential.

Even when an autoclave is set to 121°C, this does not automatically confirm that every container of medium has been adequately sterilized.

Potential issues include:

- Excessive loading that restricts steam circulation.
- Dense container arrangements that lead to uneven heating.
- Improper air removal or equipment operation.
- Using the same sterilization cycle for different medium volumes.
- Secondary contamination when filter-sterilized, heat-sensitive ingredients are added.

**Recommendation:** Validate sterilization procedures based on medium volume, container type, and equipment conditions. Include uninoculated blank medium controls to help identify possible problems during preparation, sterilization, or subsequent handling.

## 5. Overlooking Culture Room Management

Contamination prevention should continue beyond the inoculation stage.

Important aspects of culture room management include:

- Regular cleaning of culture shelves and surrounding areas.
- Prompt isolation and handling of contaminated cultures.
- Routine inspection of containers and closure conditions.
- Complete production and batch records for traceability.

It is important to distinguish environmental contamination from contamination inside a properly closed culture container.

Microorganisms present in the culture room do not necessarily enter a container when its barrier remains intact.

However, damaged containers, compromised closures, or subsequent opening procedures may introduce additional contamination risks.

**Recommendation:** Maintain a clean culture room, inspect containers regularly, handle contaminated materials promptly, and establish reliable batch management and traceability procedures.

## 6. How to Initially Trace the Source of Contamination

Different contamination patterns can provide useful clues during troubleshooting.

| Observed Situation | Suggested Priority Checks |
|---|---|
| Contamination soon after inoculation | Explant treatment, inoculation procedures, medium sterilization |
| Repeated contamination from the same plant material source | Stock plant management, sampling location, possible endophytic microorganisms |
| Different materials contaminated within the same batch | Operators, tools, workstations, and shared materials |
| Contamination in uninoculated blank medium | Medium preparation, sterilization, sealing, and subsequent handling |
| Abnormalities near the container opening or lid | Container–lid compatibility, closure condition, and handling during inoculation |
| Contamination after an extended culture period | Latent contamination, early low-level contamination, and subsequent operations |

These observations are intended as initial troubleshooting guidance rather than definitive diagnoses.

Contamination timing and location alone cannot establish the source. Production records, appropriate controls, and microbiological testing may be necessary for further investigation.

## 7. How Can Tissue Culture Laboratories Reduce Contamination Risks?

Contamination in plant tissue culture often involves multiple interacting factors.

Laboratories and commercial micropropagation facilities should consider the following practices:

1. Standardize stock plant management and explant preparation.
2. Implement consistent aseptic operating procedures.
3. Inspect culture containers, lids, and vent membranes for integrity.
4. Validate culture medium sterilization procedures and use appropriate controls.
5. Maintain culture room hygiene and reliable batch traceability.

The goal is not to rely on a single piece of equipment or consumable, but to reduce risks throughout the entire culture process.

## Conclusion

Plant tissue culture requires careful control of materials, operating conditions, and production procedures.

For research laboratories and commercial micropropagation facilities, consistent culture performance depends on reliable aseptic techniques, suitable culture containers, and effective process management.

When contamination occurs repeatedly, systematic observation, documentation, and controlled troubleshooting are often more useful than changing a single factor without identifying the underlying cause.

**Reliable tissue culture production begins with a standardized and well-managed process.**
