## 为什么有些植物组培培养基是黑色的？活性炭和香蕉在组培中的应用

前段时间，我们在走访一家植物组织培养基地时，看到了一批比较特别的培养材料。

培养瓶中的植株本身并没有什么异常，但瓶底的培养基颜色非常深，接近黑色。

我们当时看到以后，第一反应就是：

培养基里面是不是加入了活性炭？

后来和现场工作人员交流后了解到，这套培养体系中确实使用了活性炭，同时还加入了香蕉。

对于长期从事兰科植物组织培养的人来说，这样的配方可能并不陌生。

活性炭在植物组织培养中的应用范围比较广，而香蕉匀浆等天然有机添加物也可以在部分兰科植物培养体系中见到。

那么，为什么要在植物组织培养基中加入这些成分？

## 一、为什么有些植物组培培养基呈黑色？

看到黑色或明显变深的组培培养基时，活性炭通常是首先值得考虑的原因之一。

活性炭本身会使培养基明显变暗，因此加入以后，原本颜色较浅的培养基可能变成深灰色甚至接近黑色。

但需要明确一点：

培养基变黑只是加入活性炭以后产生的外观变化，并不是使用活性炭的目的。

活性炭真正重要的特性是：

较强的吸附能力。

活性炭具有丰富的孔隙结构和较大的内部表面积，可以吸附培养基中的多种物质。

这也是它长期以来被用于部分植物组织培养体系的重要原因。

## 二、活性炭在植物组织培养中有什么作用？

植物材料在离体培养过程中可能释放酚类物质及其他代谢物。

在一些培养体系中，这些物质的积累可能对培养材料产生不利影响。

活性炭能够吸附部分抑制性物质、酚类分泌物及其他代谢产物，因此在某些植物和培养阶段中，可以对培养过程产生积极作用。

相关综述显示，活性炭已经被应用于微繁殖、兰花种子萌发、体细胞胚发生、生根以及其他多种植物离体培养体系。

因此，我们不能简单地把活性炭理解成：

“让培养基变黑的材料。”

它实际上会改变培养基内部的化学环境。

## 三、但活性炭并不是加得越多越好

这可能是理解活性炭时更加重要的一点。

因为活性炭具有很强的吸附能力，所以它不会只吸附我们“不希望存在”的物质。

它同样可能吸附培养基中的：

植物生长调节剂、部分维生素以及某些金属离子等成分。

早期研究已经观察到活性炭可以吸附培养基中的生长素和细胞分裂素；后续综述也指出，这种吸附作用可能明显改变培养基中某些成分实际能够被植物利用的水平。

所以：

Activated charcoal is not simply a “more is better” additive.

是否加入活性炭、加入多少以及在哪一个培养阶段使用，都需要根据具体植物和培养体系进行调整。

同一种浓度，在一种植物上表现良好，并不意味着可以直接复制到另外一种植物。

## 四、为什么植物组织培养基里还会加入香蕉？

这次走访中另外一个让我们感兴趣的地方，就是现场人员告诉我们：

培养基里还加入了香蕉。

对于没有接触过兰科组培的人来说，这听起来可能有些特别。

但在部分兰科植物的组织培养体系中，香蕉并不是一种完全陌生的添加物。

实际应用中通常不是简单地把一块香蕉放入培养瓶，而是将其处理成香蕉匀浆（Banana Homogenate）或类似形式，作为天然有机添加物加入培养基。

香蕉可以向培养体系带入糖类、矿物质、维生素以及多种复杂的天然有机成分。

因此，在一些兰科植物的培养体系中，它会作为一种复合型有机添加物使用。

## 五、为什么香蕉在兰科组培中比较常见？

兰科植物组织培养中使用天然有机添加物已经有较长历史。

除了香蕉之外，不同培养体系中还可以见到椰子水、马铃薯等天然来源的有机添加物。

这类材料的特点是：

它们并不是单一化学成分。

以香蕉为例，它提供的是一个复杂的天然成分组合，而不是某一种单独的植物生长调节剂。

因此，它在不同兰科植物、不同培养阶段和不同基础培养基中的表现可能并不完全一致。

这也是为什么不能简单总结成：

“加香蕉，植物就一定长得更好。”

更准确的理解应该是：

香蕉匀浆是部分植物组织培养体系中可以使用的一种天然有机添加物。

具体是否适合，需要通过相应植物的培养体系和实际试验来确定。

## 六、“香蕉 + 活性炭”同时使用罕见吗？

从植物组织培养行业来看，并不能算一种特别罕见的组合。

尤其是在部分兰科植物培养体系中，可以看到天然有机添加物与活性炭同时使用。

所以我们这次在基地现场看到以后，最初觉得比较特别；但进一步查阅资料后会发现：

对于长期从事兰科组培的人来说，这可能是一种相对熟悉的培养思路。

不过，这并不意味着：

兰科组培 = 香蕉 + 活性炭。

这是需要特别避免的误解。

不同兰科植物之间存在差异；

同一种植物的不同培养阶段也存在差异；

不同实验室使用的基础培养基、生长调节剂以及其他添加物同样可能不同。

因此，有些体系使用香蕉和活性炭；

有些只使用其中一种；

也有很多培养体系完全不采用这样的组合。

## 七、为什么不同组培实验室的培养基差异这么大？

这其实是我们在实际走访植物组织培养基地时，经常觉得很有意思的一点。

大家做的都是“植物组织培养”，但真正进入商业化生产以后，不同实验室之间的培养体系可能存在明显差异。

因为植物离体培养最终受到很多因素共同影响：

植物种类

外植体类型

培养阶段

基础培养基

植物生长调节剂

糖源和有机添加物

温度

光照

气体交换

培养容器

培养周期

等等。

因此，一套在某种植物上表现良好的培养基，并不能简单复制到所有植物上。

商业化组培实验室通常需要经过长期试验和生产实践，逐渐形成适合自身植物材料和生产流程的培养体系。

## 八、从一瓶黑色培养基，我们真正看到的是什么？

我们最开始注意到这些培养瓶，其实只是因为一个很简单的问题：

“为什么培养基是黑色的？”

但继续了解以后，这个问题就变得更加有意思。

黑色让我们注意到活性炭；

与现场工作人员交流以后，又知道培养体系中使用了香蕉；

进一步查阅资料，又会发现不同植物、不同实验室在这些添加物的使用方式上存在很大差异。

这其实也是植物组织培养非常有意思的一点：

外观看起来相似的培养瓶，里面运行的可能是完全不同的培养体系。

培养容器只是整个培养环境的一部分。

真正的植物组织培养体系，是由：

植物材料 + 培养基 + 容器 + 气体交换 + 光照 + 温度 + 培养管理

共同组成的。

## 结语

在植物组织培养中看到黑色培养基，并不意味着遇到了一种非常特殊的培养技术。

如果培养基中加入了活性炭，它本身就会使培养基颜色明显变深。

而活性炭真正重要的价值，在于它的吸附特性；与此同时，这种吸附也可能影响培养基中的植物生长调节剂及其他成分，因此不能脱离具体培养体系使用。

香蕉匀浆则属于天然有机添加物，在部分兰科植物组织培养体系中可以见到。

所以真正值得理解的，并不是简单记住：

“黑色培养基 = 活性炭 + 香蕉。”

而是：

植物组织培养没有适用于所有植物的统一培养基配方。

不同植物、不同培养阶段以及不同商业化生产体系，都可能形成自己的培养方案。

而这些在真实生产现场看到的差异，也正是我们持续走访和了解植物组织培养行业时，觉得非常有价值的地方。

## Why Are Some Tissue Culture Media Black? Activated Charcoal and Banana in Plant Tissue Culture

During a recent visit to a plant tissue culture facility, we noticed something interesting.

The plantlets themselves did not appear unusual.

What immediately caught our attention was the culture medium at the bottom of the vessels.

It was very dark—almost black.

Our first thought was:

Does the medium contain activated charcoal?

After speaking with the team at the facility, we learned that activated charcoal was indeed used in this culture system, together with banana as an organic additive.

For professionals working with orchid tissue culture, this may not be particularly surprising.

Activated charcoal has been used across a range of plant tissue culture applications, while banana homogenate and other natural organic supplements can also be found in some orchid culture protocols.

So why are these ingredients added to tissue culture media?

## 1. Why Are Some Plant Tissue Culture Media Black?

When a tissue culture medium appears very dark or almost black, activated charcoal is one of the first possible causes to consider.

Adding activated charcoal significantly darkens the medium.

However, an important distinction should be made:

Darkening the medium is an effect of activated charcoal, not the primary reason for using it.

The more important property of activated charcoal is its:

high adsorption capacity.

Activated charcoal has an extensive porous structure and large internal surface area, allowing it to adsorb a variety of substances from the culture medium.

This property explains much of its long-standing use in plant tissue culture.

## 2. What Does Activated Charcoal Do in Plant Tissue Culture?

Plant tissues maintained under in vitro conditions may release phenolic compounds and other metabolites into the culture environment.

In certain culture systems, the accumulation of these substances can negatively affect the cultured material.

Activated charcoal can adsorb some inhibitory compounds, phenolic exudates and other metabolites.

Its use has been reported across a wide range of in vitro applications, including micropropagation, orchid seed germination, somatic embryogenesis and rooting.

Activated charcoal should therefore not be viewed simply as:

“the material that makes tissue culture medium black.”

Its presence can substantially alter the chemical environment of the culture medium.

## 3. Why Is More Activated Charcoal Not Necessarily Better?

This is one of the most important points when understanding activated charcoal in plant tissue culture.

Activated charcoal does not selectively adsorb only substances that we want to remove.

It may also adsorb useful components of the culture medium, including:

plant growth regulators, certain vitamins and some metal ions.

Earlier experimental work demonstrated adsorption of auxins and cytokinins from tissue culture media, while broader reviews have documented the complex effects of activated charcoal on medium composition.

Therefore:

Activated charcoal is not a “more is better” additive.

Whether it should be used, at what concentration, and at which culture stage depends on the plant species and the specific culture protocol.

A concentration that performs well in one system should not automatically be transferred to another.

## 4. Why Is Banana Added to Tissue Culture Media?

The second interesting detail from our visit was that the facility also used banana in the culture medium.

For someone unfamiliar with orchid tissue culture, adding banana to laboratory culture media may sound unusual.

In practice, banana is generally processed into banana homogenate or a similar preparation and incorporated into the medium as a natural organic supplement.

Banana can introduce a complex mixture of carbohydrates, minerals, vitamins and other naturally occurring organic compounds into the culture system.

For this reason, banana homogenate has been used as an organic supplement in some orchid tissue culture protocols.

## 5. Why Is Banana Associated Particularly with Orchid Tissue Culture?

Natural organic supplements have a long history of use in orchid tissue culture.

Depending on the culture system, materials such as banana, coconut water and other natural supplements may be incorporated into the medium.

One important characteristic of these additives is that:

they are chemically complex rather than single defined compounds.

Banana homogenate, for example, provides a mixture of naturally occurring components rather than one isolated plant growth regulator.

Its effects can therefore vary depending on the orchid species, culture stage, basal medium and other components of the protocol.

This is why it would be inaccurate to say:

“Adding banana always improves tissue culture growth.”

A better interpretation is:

Banana homogenate is one possible natural organic supplement used in certain plant tissue culture systems.

Its suitability needs to be evaluated within a specific protocol.

## 6. Is the Combination of Banana and Activated Charcoal Unusual?

Not necessarily.

The combined use of natural organic supplements and activated charcoal can be found in some orchid tissue culture systems.

What we observed during the facility visit was therefore not an entirely unusual or unknown technique.

For professionals who routinely work with orchid micropropagation, it may be relatively familiar.

However, this does not mean:

Orchid tissue culture = banana + activated charcoal.

Different orchid genera and species can respond differently.

Different culture stages may require different formulations.

Basal media, plant growth regulators and other additives also vary between laboratories and protocols.

Some systems may use both banana and activated charcoal.

Others may use only one of them.

Many tissue culture protocols use neither.

## 7. Why Do Tissue Culture Media Vary So Much Between Laboratories?

This was perhaps the most interesting lesson from what initially looked like a simple black culture medium.

Plant tissue culture is influenced by many interacting factors, including:

plant species

explant type

culture stage

basal medium

plant growth regulators

carbon sources and organic supplements

temperature

lighting

gas exchange

culture vessel

culture duration

and many other variables.

A medium that performs well for one plant cannot simply be assumed to work equally well for another.

Commercial micropropagation laboratories therefore often refine their protocols through repeated trials and production experience.

Over time, each operation may develop culture systems suited to its own plant materials and production requirements.

## 8. What Can a Black Culture Medium Tell Us?

At first, our question was very simple:

“Why is this culture medium black?”

The dark appearance led us to activated charcoal.

Speaking with the team revealed that banana was also incorporated into the culture system.

Looking deeper into the subject then revealed how differently these additives can be used across species and protocols.

This illustrates something important about plant tissue culture:

Two culture vessels may look similar from the outside while containing very different culture systems inside.

The vessel itself is only one component of the in vitro environment.

A complete tissue culture system is created by the interaction of:

Plant Material + Culture Medium + Vessel + Gas Exchange + Light + Temperature + Culture Management

## Conclusion

A black tissue culture medium does not necessarily indicate an unusual or highly specialized technique.

When activated charcoal is incorporated into the medium, significant darkening is an expected visual effect.

The more important reason for using activated charcoal is its adsorption capacity. At the same time, that adsorption can also affect plant growth regulators and other medium components, which is why activated charcoal must be evaluated within the specific culture system rather than treated as a universally beneficial additive.

Banana homogenate is a different type of additive—a natural organic supplement that can be found in some orchid tissue culture protocols.

The key lesson is therefore not:

“Black medium = activated charcoal + banana.”

It is:

There is no single tissue culture medium formulation that is ideal for every plant.

Different species, culture stages and commercial production systems can require very different protocols.

For us, these differences are one of the most interesting things to observe when visiting real commercial plant tissue culture facilities.
