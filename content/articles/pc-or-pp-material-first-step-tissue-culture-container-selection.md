## PC还是PP？商业化组培选择培养容器时，材质其实只是第一步

在选择植物组织培养容器时，我们经常遇到一个问题：

**PC组培容器和PP组培容器，到底哪一种更好？**

如果只从材料性能来看，这个问题似乎并不难回答。

PC（聚碳酸酯）透明度高、刚性好、抗冲击性能优异，适合长期重复使用；PP（聚丙烯）成本更有优势，耐热性良好，而且可以做成多种不同结构的培养容器。

但在实际接触不同商业化组培基地以后，我们发现：

**真正决定一种培养容器是否好用的，往往不只是PC还是PP。**

容器的开口大小、整体尺寸、操作习惯、培养架和组培筐的空间利用、清洗与周转方式，以及瓶盖和透气结构，都可能影响最终的使用体验。

所以，与其简单地问：

**“PC和PP哪个好？”**

不如先问：

**“哪一种容器更适合我的生产方式？”**

---

## 一、先看材料：PC和PP确实存在明显差异

PC和PP都是植物组织培养中常见的塑料材料，但两者的材料特性并不相同。

### PC组培容器

PC具有较高的透明度，方便观察瓶内培养物的生长状态。

同时，PC具有良好的刚性和抗冲击性能。在需要长期清洗、灭菌、装筐、搬运和重复周转的商业化生产环境中，这些特性具有实际意义。

在规定的使用条件下，我们目前的PC组培容器可满足最高约 **130°C** 的高温灭菌需求。

因此，PC常被用于组培瓶、组培试管以及部分广口培养杯等需要较高透明度和长期重复使用的产品。

### PP组培容器

PP的透明度通常低于PC，更常呈半透明状态，但对于许多常规培养观察需求仍然可以满足。

PP的一个明显优势是经济实用，同时具有良好的耐热性能。在规定条件下，我们目前的PP组培容器可满足约 **120°C** 的高温灭菌需求。

另外，PP非常适合制造不同尺寸和结构的培养容器，在实际产品中尤其常见各种广口培养桶、培养盒和其他容器结构。

因此：

**PC和PP首先是两种材料，但材料性能只是选型的第一层。**

---

## 二、一个容易忽略的区别：PP广口容器通常更多

如果观察实际使用中的组培容器，会发现一个很有意思的现象：

**很多PP组培容器采用广口设计。**

相比之下，PC产品中传统瓶型、试管型产品比较常见，广口培养杯虽然也有应用，但产品结构相对更集中。

需要特别说明的是：

**这并不意味着PC材料不能制造广口容器。**

它更多反映的是不同材料在实际产品开发、市场需求和长期使用中逐渐形成的产品结构差异。

而“广口”并不仅仅是外观上的区别。

瓶口大小会直接影响实际操作。

---

## 三、广口还是传统瓶型？很大程度上取决于操作习惯

对于每天进行大量接种和转接操作的组培基地来说，瓶口大小可能比很多材料参数更加直观。

广口容器可以提供更大的操作空间。

在接种、继代转接、调整培养物以及取苗时，镊子等操作工具进出容器更加方便；在后续清洗和处理培养基残留时，较大的开口在一些生产流程中也可能更加便利。

而传统瓶型同样拥有大量长期用户。

很多组培基地已经围绕某一种瓶型形成了成熟的接种动作、装筐方式、清洗流程和培养管理习惯。

因此，当一个客户告诉我们：

**“我们一直使用PP容器。”**

他真正习惯的可能并不仅仅是PP这种材料。

他可能同时习惯了：

**PP + 广口结构 + 某种容器尺寸 + 现有接种方式 + 现有周转流程。**

同样，一个长期使用PC组培瓶的基地，也可能已经围绕传统瓶型建立了非常成熟的生产体系。

所以，组培容器选型实际上也是一种**操作方式的选择**。

---

## 四、不要只比较容量，还要看容器尺寸和空间利用

另一个常见误区，是只按照容量选择培养容器。

例如：

“我们以前使用500 mL，所以新的容器也选择500 mL。”

但对于商业化组培生产来说，相同或接近容量的培养容器，实际使用效率可能完全不同。

因为还需要考虑：

- 容器底径是多少？
- 高度是多少？
- 一个组培筐能够放多少只？
- 一个培养架每层能够摆放多少只？
- 实际每个容器放多少株苗？
- 容器之间需要预留多少空间？

因此，比“单个容器容量”更值得关注的问题可能是：

**在相同的培养空间里，这套容器系统能够承载多少有效培养量？**

当生产规模达到几千、几万甚至更多培养容器时，几毫米的尺寸差异经过培养筐、培养架和整个培养室不断放大，最终可能影响空间利用和日常周转效率。

所以，选择PC还是PP时，不能脱离具体的容器尺寸和生产布局。

---

## 五、采购价格和实际使用成本，是两个不同的问题

PP组培容器通常具有较好的成本优势。

因此，当采购数量较大、预算敏感时，PP往往具有很强的吸引力。

但对于需要长期重复使用的培养容器来说，仅比较第一次采购价格并不完整。

实际使用过程中还可能涉及：

**清洗、灭菌、人工搬运、装筐、重复周转、检查和最终淘汰。**

PC良好的透明度、刚性和抗冲击能力，使它在长期重复周转体系中具有自己的优势。

PP同样可以重复使用，并且在经济性和结构选择方面具有明显价值。

所以真正应该比较的，不只是：

**“一个瓶子多少钱？”**

而是：

**“这套容器进入我的生产体系以后，整个使用周期的成本和效率怎么样？”**

换句话说：

**Purchase Price ≠ Total Cost of Use**

采购价格，并不等于完整的使用成本。

---

## 六、PC还是PP，并不能直接决定透气性能

还有一个很容易混淆的问题：

**PC和PP哪一种更透气？**

实际上，对于带透气结构的组培容器来说，这个问题不能简单由瓶体材料决定。

真正影响瓶内气体交换的因素还包括：

- 容器体积；
- 瓶内顶部空间（headspace）；
- 瓶口结构；
- 瓶盖结构；
- 透气膜材料；
- 有效透气面积；
- 瓶体与瓶盖的配合状态。

因此：

**“选择PC还是PP”和“需要怎样的气体交换”其实是两个不同的问题。**

即使两个容器使用相同材料，只要容器体积、瓶盖结构或者透气膜有效面积不同，实际培养微环境就可能不同。

所以，在商业化组培中，更合理的方式是把：

**容器 + 瓶盖 + 透气结构**

作为一套完整系统进行考虑。

---

## 七、植物种类和培养阶段，也会改变容器需求

不同植物以及不同培养阶段，对培养容器的需求并不完全相同。

有些培养体系更关注观察清晰度；

有些需要更大的操作开口；

有些需要更高的内部空间；

有些则更加关注培养密度、气体交换或者单位培养空间的利用效率。

因此，我们并不建议简单规定：

“某个培养阶段一定使用PC。”

或者：

“某一种植物一定使用PP。”

更合理的做法，是根据具体培养体系，同时考虑：

**材质、容器结构、尺寸、开口和透气方式。**

---

## 八、同样是PC或PP，实际使用表现也不一定完全相同

材料名称只是一个起点。

两个都标注“PC”的组培容器，并不意味着它们在实际生产中的表现完全一样。

两个都使用“PP”的培养容器，也可能拥有完全不同的结构和使用体验。

因为最终产品还受到很多制造和设计因素影响，例如：

**壁厚分布、成型一致性、瓶口尺寸、瓶盖配合、容器结构以及透气设计。**

对于商业化组培来说，真正使用的是一个具体的培养容器，而不是材料参数表上的“PC”或“PP”两个字母。

这也是为什么我们认为：

**材料选择很重要，但产品设计和制造同样重要。**

---

## 九、那么，PC和PP到底应该怎么选？

如果希望获得更高的透明度、良好的刚性和抗冲击能力，并计划长期重复周转，PC通常值得优先考虑。

如果更加关注经济性，同时偏好广口结构，希望拥有更多不同尺寸和结构的选择，PP往往非常实用。

但这并不是绝对规则。

真正选型时，我们更建议依次考虑：

**1. 对透明度的要求有多高？**

**2. 更习惯广口还是传统瓶型？**

**3. 接种、转接和取苗时需要多大的操作空间？**

**4. 一个组培筐和培养架能够放多少只？**

**5. 是否计划长期重复使用？**

**6. 更关注首次采购成本，还是整个使用周期的综合成本？**

**7. 需要怎样的瓶盖和透气结构？**

**8. 现有生产流程是否已经围绕某一种容器形成稳定操作习惯？**

把这些问题回答清楚以后，PC还是PP，通常就不会那么难选。

---

## 结语：最合适的容器，不一定是某项参数最好的容器

PC和PP并不是简单的“高配和低配”，也不是谁一定能够取代谁。

在商业化植物组织培养中，两种材料都有长期存在的实际应用价值。

真正的组培容器选型应该考虑：

**材质 × 开口结构 × 容器尺寸 × 操作习惯 × 空间利用 × 周转方式 × 透气系统 × 综合使用成本**

对于商业化组培来说，最合适的培养容器，往往不是某一个材料参数最漂亮的产品。

而是：

**最适合现有培养体系和生产流程的那一个。**

## PC or PP? Material Is Only the First Step When Selecting Tissue Culture Containers

One of the most common questions we hear when discussing tissue culture vessels is:

**Which is better for plant tissue culture—PC or PP?**

At first, the answer may appear to be a simple comparison of material properties.

Polycarbonate (PC) offers high optical clarity, good rigidity and excellent impact resistance, making it well suited to repeated handling and reuse. Polypropylene (PP) is economical, heat-resistant and highly versatile in container design.

In commercial micropropagation, however, material is only part of the decision.

The opening diameter, vessel dimensions, operator preferences, basket and rack utilization, washing and handling procedures, closure design and ventilation system can all affect how well a container performs in day-to-day production.

Instead of asking only:

**“Is PC or PP better?”**

A more useful question is:

**“Which vessel works better with our production system?”**

---

## 1. Start with the Material: PC and PP Do Have Different Properties

Both PC and PP are widely used for plant tissue culture containers, but their material characteristics are different.

### PC Tissue Culture Containers

PC provides high optical clarity, making it easier to observe plant growth and culture conditions inside the vessel.

It also offers good rigidity and excellent impact resistance. These properties become particularly useful when vessels are repeatedly washed, autoclaved, loaded into baskets, transported and reused in commercial production.

Under specified operating conditions, our current PC tissue culture containers are designed to withstand high-temperature sterilization at up to approximately **130°C**.

PC is therefore commonly used for tissue culture bottles, culture tubes and selected wide-mouth culture cups where optical clarity and repeated use are important.

### PP Tissue Culture Containers

PP generally offers lower optical clarity than PC and is more commonly translucent, although it still provides sufficient visibility for many routine culture applications.

One of PP's major advantages is its cost efficiency combined with good heat resistance. Under specified operating conditions, our current PP tissue culture containers are designed for sterilization temperatures of approximately **120°C**.

PP is also highly versatile in terms of product geometry and is commonly used for wide-mouth culture containers, culture boxes and other practical vessel designs.

This is the first level of comparison.

But in actual production, the decision goes much further than material properties.

---

## 2. An Often-Overlooked Difference: Wide-Mouth Designs Are More Common Among PP Vessels

Looking at the tissue culture vessels used in real production environments, one practical difference quickly becomes apparent:

**Wide-mouth designs are particularly common among PP culture containers.**

PC product ranges, by comparison, often include more conventional bottle and tube formats, with wide-mouth PC culture cups representing a smaller portion of the available designs.

This does not mean that PC cannot be manufactured as a wide-mouth vessel.

Rather, it reflects how different product designs, market requirements and established usage patterns have developed over time.

And opening diameter is more than a cosmetic difference.

It directly affects how operators work with the vessel.

---

## 3. Wide Mouth or Conventional Bottle? Operator Workflow Matters

In a commercial tissue culture facility performing large numbers of transfers every day, opening size can have a very practical effect on handling.

A wide-mouth vessel provides more working space during inoculation, subculture and plantlet retrieval.

Forceps and other tools can move through a larger opening more easily, and wider access may also simplify cleaning and removal of residual medium in some production workflows.

Conventional bottle formats, however, also have a large and established user base.

Many laboratories and commercial facilities have already built their inoculation procedures, basket configurations, washing routines and culture-management practices around a particular vessel format.

Therefore, when a customer says:

**“We have always used PP containers,”**

the preference may not be about PP alone.

What they may actually be accustomed to is:

**PP + a wide opening + a particular vessel size + an established transfer technique + an existing handling workflow.**

The same is true for facilities that have used conventional PC tissue culture bottles for many years.

This is why selecting a tissue culture vessel is not simply a material decision.

**It is also a workflow decision.**

---

## 4. Do Not Compare Volume Alone—Consider Space Utilization

Another common approach is to select vessels primarily by nominal volume.

For example:

“We currently use a 500 mL vessel, so we need another 500 mL vessel.”

In commercial micropropagation, however, two vessels with similar nominal capacities can perform very differently within the production system.

Other questions matter:

- What is the vessel footprint?
- How tall is it?
- How many vessels fit into one culture basket?
- How many fit on each rack level?
- How many plantlets are cultured in each vessel?
- How much spacing is required between vessels?

A more meaningful question may therefore be:

**How much productive culture capacity can this vessel system provide within the available growing space?**

When thousands or tens of thousands of vessels are in circulation, small differences in diameter and height can be multiplied across baskets, racks and entire culture rooms.

Material selection should therefore never be separated from vessel dimensions and production layout.

---

## 5. Purchase Price and Total Cost of Use Are Not the Same

PP vessels often provide an attractive initial cost advantage.

For high-volume purchasing and cost-sensitive applications, this can make PP a very practical choice.

However, when vessels are intended for repeated use, purchase price alone does not provide the full picture.

The production cycle may also include:

**washing, autoclaving, manual handling, basket loading, repeated circulation, inspection and eventual replacement.**

The high clarity, rigidity and impact resistance of PC can provide meaningful advantages in long-term reusable systems.

PP can also be reused and offers strong value where economical pricing and flexible vessel designs are priorities.

The more useful question is therefore not simply:

**“How much does one vessel cost?”**

but:

**“What does this vessel cost to use throughout its role in our production system?”**

In other words:

**Purchase Price ≠ Total Cost of Use**

---

## 6. PC or PP Does Not Determine Ventilation Performance by Itself

Another question that sometimes becomes mixed into the material discussion is:

**Which material provides better ventilation—PC or PP?**

For tissue culture vessels with designed ventilation, gas exchange cannot be determined by the vessel material alone.

Other important factors include:

- vessel volume;
- headspace;
- neck and opening geometry;
- closure design;
- vent membrane properties;
- effective vent area;
- fit between the vessel and closure.

This means that:

**“Should we use PC or PP?” and “How much gas exchange does our culture require?” are two different questions.**

Two vessels made from the same material can create different culture environments if their volume, closure geometry or effective ventilation area is different.

For this reason, we prefer to evaluate:

**Vessel + Closure + Ventilation**

as an integrated culture system.

---

## 7. Plant Species and Culture Stage Can Change Vessel Requirements

Different plants—and different stages of the same propagation process—may require different vessel characteristics.

Some applications place greater emphasis on optical clarity.

Others benefit from a larger working opening.

Some require additional internal height, while others prioritize culture density, gas exchange or efficient use of rack space.

For this reason, it is rarely appropriate to make universal statements such as:

“PC should always be used at this culture stage.”

or:

“This plant should always be cultured in PP.”

A better approach is to consider the specific culture system and evaluate:

**material, vessel geometry, dimensions, opening size and ventilation design together.**

---

## 8. Not All PC Vessels—or PP Vessels—Perform the Same

The material designation is only the starting point.

Two vessels both labeled “PC” do not necessarily perform identically in commercial use.

The same applies to PP vessels.

The finished product is also influenced by factors such as:

**wall-thickness distribution, molding consistency, opening dimensions, vessel–closure fit, structural design and ventilation configuration.**

Commercial tissue culture facilities ultimately work with a finished vessel—not simply with the letters “PC” or “PP” on a material specification sheet.

Material selection matters.

**Product design and manufacturing consistency matter as well.**

---

## 9. So, How Should You Choose Between PC and PP?

If high optical clarity, good rigidity, impact resistance and long-term repeated handling are priorities, PC is often worth considering.

If initial cost efficiency is a major priority, or if your workflow favors wide-mouth vessels and a broader range of container geometries, PP can be an excellent practical option.

These are guidelines rather than absolute rules.

Before choosing, consider the following questions:

1. How important is optical clarity?
2. Do operators prefer a wide-mouth vessel or a conventional bottle format?
3. How much working space is needed for inoculation, subculture and plantlet retrieval?
4. How many vessels fit into each basket and onto each culture rack?
5. Will the vessels be reused over long production cycles?
6. Is initial purchase price or total cost of use more important?
7. What closure and ventilation configuration does the culture require?
8. Is the existing production workflow already optimized around a particular vessel format?

Once these questions are answered, choosing between PC and PP becomes much easier.

---

## Conclusion: The Best Vessel Is Not Necessarily the One With the Best Individual Specification

PC and PP should not simply be viewed as a premium option and a budget option, nor should one be expected to replace the other in every application.

Both materials have established roles in commercial plant tissue culture.

A more complete selection process considers:

**Material × Opening Design × Vessel Dimensions × Operator Workflow × Space Utilization × Handling Cycle × Ventilation System × Total Cost of Use**

For commercial micropropagation, the best tissue culture vessel is often not the one with the most impressive individual material specification.

It is the one that integrates most effectively into the actual culture and production system.
